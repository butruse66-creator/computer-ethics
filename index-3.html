The Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics

A widely taught guide from the Computer Ethics Institute, summarised.

  1. Do not use a computer to harm other people.
  2. Do not interfere with other people's computer work.
  3. Do not snoop around in other people's files.
  4. Do not use a computer to steal.
  5. Do not use a computer to spread false information.
  6. Do not use or copy software you have not paid for.
  7. Do not use others' resources without permission or credit.
  8. Do not claim other people's intellectual work as your own.
  9. Think about the social impact of the programs you write.
  10. Always show consideration and respect for others.

Do & Don't

Everyday habits that put ethics into practice.

✅ Do

  • Use strong, unique passwords
  • Credit sources and creators
  • Ask permission before sharing data
  • Report security flaws responsibly
  • Verify information before posting

❌ Don't

  • Pirate software, music or films
  • Access accounts that aren't yours
  • Plagiarise or copy assignments
  • Cyberbully or spread rumours
  • Share someone's private details

Think It Through

Tap a scenario to see the ethical view.

A friend asks to copy your coding assignment

Sharing work for copying is academic dishonesty. Help them understand the concept instead of handing over your solution.

You find a bug that exposes users' data

Report it to the owner promptly and responsibly. Do not exploit it or publish it before it is fixed.

An app collects more data than it needs

Ethical design follows data minimisation: collect only what is necessary, be transparent, and get consent.

You see a viral post that looks fake

Check reliable sources before sharing. Spreading misinformation can cause real harm.
